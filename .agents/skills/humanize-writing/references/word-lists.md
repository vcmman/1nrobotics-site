## 1. Overused single words → plain swaps

These appear far more in post-2022 machine text than in comparable human writing. Swap for the plain equivalent when the plain word works.

| showcase | show, display, present |

Sentence-initial defaults to vary or drop: **Additionally,** **Moreover,** **Furthermore,** **Notably,** **Importantly.**

## 2. Significance / legacy / "broader trends" phrases

Reflexive importance-inflation. The strongest single category of tell.

*stands as / serves as; is a testament to; is a reminder of; plays a vital / significant / crucial / pivotal / key role; marks / marking a pivotal moment; represents / marks a shift; a key turning point; underscores / highlights its importance / significance; reflects a broader; symbolizing its ongoing / enduring / lasting; contributing to the; setting the stage for; shaping the; an evolving landscape; a focal point; left an indelible mark; deeply rooted; cements its place / status; solidified its role; rich (cultural) heritage; rich history; storied tradition.*

Fix: state the fact. Drop the claim about what it "represents" unless a source makes it and it's relevant.

## 3. Superficial-analysis participles (tail clauses)

A fact, then a comma, then a present participle that fakes analysis:

*…, highlighting …; …, underscoring …; …, emphasizing …; …, reflecting …; …, symbolizing …; …, showcasing …; …, ensuring …; …, fostering …; …, cultivating …; …, encompassing …; …, contributing to …; …, cementing its role as …; …, paving the way for …; …, serving as a …; …, marking a ….*

Fix: split into a real sentence with a concrete sourced claim, or delete.

## 4. Promotional / puffery vocabulary

Brochure and travel-guide register that leaks in even when neutrality is requested:

*boasts a; vibrant; rich; profound; enhancing; showcasing; exemplifies; commitment to; natural beauty; nestled; in the heart of; groundbreaking; renowned; world-class; state-of-the-art; cutting-edge; featuring; a diverse array of; stunning; breathtaking; must-see; iconic; unparalleled; treasure trove; hidden gem; bustling; charming; picturesque.*

Note: newer models tend to be *subtly* positive rather than using blatant superlatives like "the best" — so watch for soft, pervasive enthusiasm, not just obvious peacock words.

## 5. Vague-attribution (weasel) phrases

Opinions pinned to an unnamed authority:

*experts argue / say; observers have noted / cited; critics argue; some critics argue; industry reports suggest; it is widely regarded / believed; many believe; it is often said; researchers and conservationists; scholars describe; several sources / publications* (when few or none are cited); *some have described; is considered by many; is seen as.*

Fix: name the source, state plainly as fact if uncontested, or cut.

## 6. Negative-parallelism templates

*not only … but also …; it's not just … it's …; isn't merely … it's …; not X, but rather Y; more than just …; it's less about … and more about …; rather than … , it ….*

Fix: assert the positive directly. Cap at one such construction per piece.

## 6b. Structural-triple templates

Rule-of-three patterns built from parallel phrases rather than adjectives — these survive rewording, so change the *count*, not just the words:

*whether you're a …, a …, or a …; another … , another … , another …; no …, no …, no …; from … to … to …; the …, the …, and the ….*

## 7. Default transitions to vary

LLMs lean on a small rotation. Mix them, or cut them — prose doesn't need a connective on every sentence:

*Additionally, Moreover, Furthermore, However, Notably, Importantly, In addition, On the other hand, As a result, Consequently, Ultimately, In conclusion, Overall, That said, It's worth noting that.*

## 8. Conversational / register-leak phrases

Chatbot-to-user habits that must not appear in finished text:

*Certainly!; Of course!; Sure!; Absolutely!; Great question!; You're absolutely right!; I hope this helps; Hope that helps!; Let me know if …; Feel free to …; Would you like …; Is there anything else …; Here's a …; Here is a quick breakdown; Let's dive in; In summary,; To summarize,; In conclusion,* (as a tacked-on recap); *I'd be happy to …; As an AI / As a large language model; In this article we will …; In this section we will explore ….*

## 9. Knowledge-cutoff disclaimer phrases

*As of my last update; up to my last training update; as of my last knowledge update; as of [date]* (when not a deliberate "as of" qualifier); *while specific details are limited / scarce; not widely available / documented / disclosed; based on the available information; in the provided / available sources; from the search results provided; I don't have access to real-time …; information may have changed since.*

Fix: delete entirely. If recency genuinely matters, state the actual date of the fact, not a disclaimer about your training.

## 10. Placeholder / template fragments

Never ship these:

*[insert date]; [Company Name]; [Your Name]; [add citation]; [citation needed] (when you wrote it, not quoting); (Add more detail here); (Expand as needed); [TBD]; XXX; lorem ipsum; [link]; [source].*
